WHAT THE TEST EXECUTES AND ASSERTS

The fixture uses a real HMAC-bound challenge, SessionRequest parsing, a locally
generated EIP-712 voucher signature, signature verification, the public
Mpp::verify_session entrypoint, and ABI decoding of an Alloy in-process mock
getChannel response. It then applies the bundled multi-fetch example's
non-management-success predicate.

Values asserted by the test:

  authenticated challenge amount / advertised page price P: 10,000
  prior accepted cumulative authorization: 10,000
  prior durable spent: 10,000
  unused prior authorization: 0
  presented cumulative authorization: 10,001
  accepted increment d: 1
  authorization remaining after verification: 1
  non-management content predicate: success
  final durable spent: 10,000 (unchanged)

The demonstrated accounting mismatch is underpayment relative to the advertised
price: P-d = 9,999 units, or 99.99% of the 10,000-unit advertised price for the
single modeled response. This is not evidence of the merchant's operating
cost, margin, or realized financial loss.

REPEATABILITY BOUND

The one-response mismatch above is executed. Static source inspection suggests
that a payer with sufficient deposit headroom can present successively higher
valid vouchers and repeat the pattern when min_voucher_delta remains below the
route price. That repetition was not exercised by this fixture and remains an
inference subject to concurrency, rate limits, top-up policy, and application
controls.

LIMITATIONS AND SAFETY BOUNDARY

  simple non-management success predicate. It does not launch Axum or invoke
  the example's actual HTTP handler over a socket.

  it does not establish that every production or third-party integration uses
  this pattern.
- The bundled SSE and WebSocket paths debit per event/frame and were not shown
  affected.
- Applications that derive a server-owned route cost and atomically debit that
