VOUCHER ENFORCEABILITY QUALIFICATION

A signed voucher is only potentially settleable when its channel was actually
opened on chain, the voucher matches that channel and authorized signer, the
channel remains active/nonfinalized, and the cumulative amount satisfies the
escrow's deposit/prior-settlement checks. Finalization/deletion ends this
enforceability. Neither local PoC submits an open or settlement transaction.

LIMITATIONS AND SAFETY BOUNDARY

- The PoCs compose two local behaviors; they do not perform one end-to-end
  open, stream, settlement, or balance-change sequence on a chain.
- The client PoC constructs but does not submit the channel-open transaction.
  Its voucher is not enforceable without a matching active/nonfinalized channel.
- The settlement PoC stubs every chain boundary. It proves the amount selected
  by settle(), not acceptance or transfer by a deployed escrow.
- The passive 0xef marker is not deployed-bytecode identity evidence.
- mpp-rs does not commit Cargo.lock at the pinned revision, so Rust dependency
  resolution can drift; the exact compiler and validation time above bound the
  recorded execution.
- Public source silence does not establish that Tempo is unaware of this issue.
- No live payment, transaction, credential use, deceptive interaction,
  production mutation, load test, or unrelated third-party test was performed.
